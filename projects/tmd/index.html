Mutation by Natural Dominance

Which evolutionary route occurs depends on more than the supply of mutations. TMD explores how context, accessibility, establishment and observation affect the routes we see, with public calculations and tests that can be inspected.

What the public work reports

Public reporter measurements, reproduced

The licensed figure-data archive for Farr and colleagues' SBW25 hotspot study is now acquired and checksum verified. Original code reconstructs 24 culture occurrences and passes 986 source arithmetic, formula and mapping checks. The pooled corrected terminal-frequency ratio between reporter backgrounds is about 118.97. The authors' reported MSS mutation-rate point estimates instead imply 58.33. These are different quantities: mutant descendants and observation affect terminal frequencies. The mutation-rate fit and its intervals were not reproduced.

Keep the assay and sampling gaps visible

The transcript reconstruction gives a 5.9603 geometric-mean ratio of regional transcript proxies under the supplied ideal-doubling transform; it does not calibrate native mutation supply. One culture row lists seven counted candidates but eight sequenced colonies, and the first sequencing occasion has 94 available reads against 95 workbook entries. These are open sampling and provenance questions. Ten measurement-contract gaps remain; colonies, sequence reads and repeated transformants cannot be relabelled as independent Wsp/Aws/Mws trials.

What a supply-transfer assumption would change

An ordinary algebraic calculation shows how prespecified native/reporter supply-error bounds would widen the existing route-contrast intervals together with recovery uncertainty. The dataset supplies no finite native/reporter bound for the three routes. Without such a justified bound, the calculation supplies no biological test. It establishes neither a new theorem nor an additional causal dominance mechanism.

Certified comparisons of frozen forecasts

A new engine gives rigorously bounded score comparisons for prespecified forecasts of observed qualifying Wsp/Aws/Mws outcomes. One simultaneous confidence region supports every frozen comparator. Twenty synthetic tests and 30,887 independent exact review assertions passed; the stored scoring example is inconclusive. These checks verify the bounded numerical procedure; independent founders, justified count laws and predictions fixed before outcomes still need a real study contract.

A precision limit, before outcomes

A conservative illustration targeting a 0.05-nat interval half-width requires 527 and 921 qualifying outcomes in its two contexts, exceeding the prototype's 500-unit per-context cap. This is a bound on interval width, not a power result or a recommended biological sample size.

New source checks strengthen competing explanations

Full-text inspection of Farr and colleagues (2025), Barber and Couce (2026), and Li and Gyorgy (2025) examines transcription-dependent mutation supply, population history and plasmid masking. Farr's engineered SBW25 reporter shows about a 58-fold difference in reported fluctuation-rate estimates across the compared backgrounds; that does not calibrate native Wsp/Aws/Mws supply. The review separates mutation production from establishment and phenotypic visibility. The Farr figure-data archive is now audited separately above; the Barber/Couce and Li/Gyorgy datasets remain unacquired. No complete matched Wsp/Aws/Mws panel is admitted within the inspected scope.

Recovery uncertainty, including all-success controls

Methods extension 0.5.0 adds conservative confidence bounds for recovery-corrected Wsp/Aws/Mws route comparisons. All-success controls still leave uncertainty; observed zeros can produce unbounded ranges. The package includes 13 automated tests and 160 synthetic studies. No complete matched biological route/control panel has been authenticated within the inspected material.

Correcting the source record

The Wsp/Aws/Mws counts 46/41/18 in Lind et al. (2019) came from separate pathway-isolated strains. They cannot be treated as competing-route winner frequencies.

Context in published observations

A secondary analysis restores the three experimental blocks in the published rpoB table. Medium-specific predictions outperform a pooled prediction in each held-out block, with an exploratory total gain of 13.126 bits across 111 isolates. This does not identify the proposed TMD mechanism.

A test of changing route fractions

A context-stratified timing diagnostic accounts for right censoring. Selected synthetic null scenarios rejected 12/200 and 9/200 datasets at a 5% threshold; a deliberately strong switching alternative was detected in 200/200. These are simulations of a procedure, not biological experiments.

A limit on identifying mechanisms

The same Lomax first-event waiting-time distribution and constant route shares can arise from different rate constructions. First-event observations alone cannot distinguish those mechanisms.

Latest GitHub research checkpoint

The recovery-transport follow-up combines the earlier sensitivity analysis with certified confidence bounds. It reuses all 160 published synthetic studies and reports 44 new passing tests. The chosen recovery bounds are hypothetical sensitivity settings, rather than measured biological recovery errors.

Two subsequent source-audit rounds are complete. The first checks census measurements and the distinction between evolutionary histories, cultures and technical plate counts; it does not estimate biological drift or establishment for TMD. The second authenticates four primary articles and records sampling units, reused observations and missing measurements. No complete matched Wsp/Aws/Mws panel is admitted within that inspected scope. This is an eligibility result, not a test rejecting or confirming TMD.

The third round completes a prospective prediction design as a design-only candidate. It specifies how to reserve an entire context, freeze predictions before selected outcomes, and compare independently measured mutation supply, establishment and recovery explanations. Its forecast concerns observed qualifying Wsp/Aws/Mws outcomes; biological interpretation additionally requires justified recovery controls and their transfer to the experiment. Twenty-six required study inputs remain unresolved, including genotypes, sampling frames, contexts, sample sizes, stopping rules and decision thresholds. All design checks use synthetic inputs; no biological experiment or registered study is claimed.

The latest dataset audit preserves the culture/transformant/occasion hierarchy, nested colony confirmations and missing or unresolved sequencing outcomes. It strengthens a concrete competing mutation-supply explanation, while leaving native-site supply, establishment and recovery transfer unresolved. No actual study-registry input is filled from the selected reporter endpoints.

A bounded changed-source watch reads two newly indexed PubMed abstracts and their metadata: a eukaryote mutation-accumulation study published on 5 October and an E.coli–P1 gene-exchange study dated 3 October. Neither full text nor underlying dataset was acquired. They motivate separate checks of mutation measurement and horizontal inheritance; their abstract claims are not independently reproduced observations or transferable SBW25 parameters.

What natural dominance means here

TMD describes a route as more naturally dominant in a specified context when it has a higher rate of producing a defined successful heritable outcome. Mutation supply, accessibility and establishment can all contribute. This differs from genetic dominance in a heterozygote. Observed winner frequencies alone cannot separate these causes, selection, drift or recovery bias. The empirical question is whether a prespecified cross-context restriction predicts held-out observations after independently measured supply and justified recovery correction. A fitted route preference alone does not establish a new causal mechanism.

Assumptions and open questions

The timing diagnostic requires independent units, independent censoring conditional on context, exact first successful arrival times and time bins chosen before inspecting outcomes. Endpoint counts in the rpoB table do not satisfy those timing requirements. Failure to reject constant route shares does not prove TMD. The newer confidence method also requires justified count laws, introduced control totals, prespecified sampling and evidence that relative control recovery transfers to biological samples. Overlapping intervals do not establish equivalence or identify a mechanism. Certified forecast scores additionally require a complete prespecified endpoint ledger and justified conditional count laws, with Other, no-qualified-outcome, unresolved and missing outcomes retained. Zero qualifying outcomes are unevaluable; correlated descendants or forecasts fitted after outcomes do not satisfy the current contract.
