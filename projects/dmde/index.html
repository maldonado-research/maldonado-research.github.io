Dark matter · dark energy

Dark Matter–Dark Energy

How could we test a hypothesis about dark matter and dark energy? DMDE designs a test using two fixed particle-injection scenarios. A future calculation would follow their neutrinos, neutron–proton reactions and the formation of helium and deuterium.

Research status

A working hypothesis and methods/software release, without peer review. No validated production predictions for helium abundance, deuterium abundance or effective neutrino number are supplied.

What the public work reports

A fixed pair of sources

Two byte-frozen, blinded spectral-source payloads share timing and neutrino shapes while differing in amplitude. The source-only analysis describes their differential-response geometry and conditional precision requirements.

A stronger chain of checks

Public validators, schemas and provider contracts cover source shape, timing, transport stepping, nuclear-input consumption and meaningful refinement. A five-process weak-rate audit includes charged-lepton capture and finite-temperature neutron-decay blocking.

Preserved public materials

The provider directory preserves 74 files from the public provider archive byte for byte. The release directory contains the five published artifacts. The frozen release’s source checks do not run neutrino transport or a Big Bang nucleosynthesis calculation.

Numerical controls for the next test

The 1 October 2026 supplement calculates the known missing inverse weak reaction and runs an established program’s ordinary Standard Model control. A separate computational review checks the equations and recorded outputs. These controls help prepare the future particle-injection calculation; they supply no DMDE abundance predictions and are not external peer review.

Assumptions and open questions

Production momentum-dependent transport, corrected weak rates, an instrumented Big Bang nucleosynthesis calculation and independent backend replication remain pending. The five exported weak histories do not separately represent the inverse three-body process; the Born-reference audit is not a complete precision treatment. Source-pair identities do not determine the final abundance response. The approximately twenty-parts-per-million example is a conditional design target, not achieved accuracy or a detection. The wider microscopic model and its dark-energy connection need separate derivations and tests.
